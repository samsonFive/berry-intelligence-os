# Local News translation integrity

The first scheduled global-expansion run exposed concrete local-model errors: two Arabic headlines changed 3,800 tons into 300, Spanish blueberry wording became cranberry, and Melipilla became Melilla. Six affected public title/summary cache entries received an audited assistant source-language correction. Original records, source text, crop assignments, relevance decisions and trust remained unchanged. This was presentation review, not human publication or Atomic Evidence approval, and used zero provider API calls.

The general repair validates local translations before caching and again when cached English is displayed. Numeric quantities, percentages, signs and explicit currency markers must agree with the original, allowing common locale-specific separators, equivalent million/billion scales and English month names. Unsupported or uncertain conversions keep the original visible with translation pending. A declared publisher credit is removed before local inference so a source name such as Hora 13 Noticias is not mistaken for an article quantity; attribution remains in the source field. HTML entities are decoded without deleting ordinary numeric comparisons.

Source-grounded blueberry terminology is kept consistent with the existing crop vocabulary. Explicit red-cranberry wording is excluded from the correction, and no crop identity is inferred from the English output. Known regional place spellings are preserved; an uncertain place change holds the translation. Dominant Arabic script is not labeled English when language identification is uncertain. These checks do not establish complete semantic accuracy, independently verify a publisher's quantities, infer a currency from a country, translate article bodies or change claim-review gates.

Cached validation is pure and bounded, makes no inference or requests, and preserves stored cache entries and originals. The live-corpus engineering replay accepted all 38 currently translated public records after documented formatting normalization and the six source-language corrections. Consumed cases and synthetic quantity regressions are not independent translation-quality or coverage scores. Unmeasured semantic errors, unsupported languages/formatting and source-body translation remain open.
