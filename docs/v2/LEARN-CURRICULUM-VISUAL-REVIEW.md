# Visual explanations throughout the existing Learn curriculum

All 27 existing lessons now have an original interactive explanation: the
existing cane-year diagram, four earlier teaching explorations, and 22 new
explorations. Five new schematic designs explain wax/light, a raised bed,
the root zone, a trellis and compression; the root drawing serves two lessons.
Native buttons select a short explanation. All explanations remain readable
without JavaScript; diagrams have text alternatives and keyboard focus.

This closes the visual-explanation gap in this bounded curriculum. It does
not establish a comprehensive educational library or a scientific re-review
of every sentence in the older lessons. The three existing video links remain;
this change does not claim a new video for every lesson.

## What the reader gains

The new illustrations explain mechanisms and comparisons, not measured crop
performance. New source links point to specific extension guidance, research
or program descriptions rather than institution homepages. The new explanations
retain the crop, region, method and source period where those affect meaning.
Older UC Davis handling sheets are labeled with their 1996/1998 dates; their
qualitative framework is not represented as current product instructions.

Blueberry bloom means wax on the fruit, not flowering. A wax/light schematic
makes that distinction explicit. A second verified photograph is displayed in
the Bloom and Color lessons with its creator and public-domain basis beside it:
[Scott Bauer, USDA ARS K5182-18](https://www.ars.usda.gov/oc/images/photos/k5182-18/),
with the [file-specific Commons rights record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blueberries.jpg).
The original 640 by 418 image loads without alteration. It does not establish
cultivar identity, wax microstructure, residue identity or a quality measurement.
Existing unverified images remain source links; the earlier Raspberry photograph
keeps its own CC BY 2.5 credit. No publisher graphic is copied into the app.

Double cropping has two different uses: a follow-up crop on reused strawberry
beds, and early/late fruiting potential in some primocane-fruiting blackberries.
The original lesson conflated them. A presentation clarification applies only
when both original summary and definition still match exactly. The stored fields
remain unchanged and their earlier wording is available in a collapsed note.
If a user has edited either field, the clarification does not replace its visible
wording. The comparison and its specific sources remain available alongside it.

The new firmness diagram cites an ARS compression study, rather than a paper
about strawberry sweetness. Texture uses the actual blueberry sensory study;
the Korean strawberry VOC study belongs to the aroma explanation. Original
record citations remain preserved in provenance. These new links support their
bounded teaching point, not every legacy statement on a page.
