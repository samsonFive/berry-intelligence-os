04

Trusted intelligence lifecycle

Review the source and its individual claims as separate decisions.

  1. Collect

    An article, filing or page is discovered, with its source and capture history available for triage.

  2. Review the publication

    A human decides whether the material belongs in the published corpus. Rejection retains the original source history.

  3. Review its statements

    Compare each proposed claim with the exact source wording. Decide each proposition separately; reviewing the article does not verify every assertion.

  4. Interpret

    Use supported statements and sources to assess patterns. Signals, assessments and educational research keep their distinct purposes.

Reading & relevance

Useful, not relevant, saved and read are personal triage decisions.

Statement triage

Keeping wording, editing and setting priority prepare a statement for review. Confirm it individually against its source after publication review.

Interpretation review

Confirming a captured development records your judgment on that interpretation. It does not publish its underlying claims.

Variety identity

A candidate may be distinct, an alias or unresolved. Reviewing a candidate does not automatically create a canonical variety or establish a breeder role.

What do the status labels mean?
Discovered
Collected material awaiting review.
Review required
A human decision is still needed.
Trusted / reviewed publication
Accepted published material; its individual claims may still need review.
AI proposed
A machine suggestion, with its sources and limitations retained.
Candidate / possible alias
A proposed identity or match, pending a human decision.
Operator action
A collection or source problem, separate from intelligence review.
When original article text is missing

Source authenticity compares a recovered copy with the original title, URL, date and text. A human accepts the copy before it can supply original text in the private reader and support variety-name discovery. Accepting text does not approve identities or individual statements. Extraction requires its own authorization.

05

From a source name to a variety profile

A name found in an article or portfolio reaches review first. It becomes a catalog entry through a separate human authoring step.

  1. 1

    Read the source

    Open the original article, breeder page or filing. Check the named crop, selection code and date. If text is unavailable, inspect a recovered copy through source authenticity review.

    A name with its original source
  2. 2

    Check the identity

    Compare the candidate with existing names, codes and aliases. A human keeps it unresolved, rejects it or marks it distinct. A company named beside it is still a source association.

    An identity decision
  3. 3

    Create the entry

    After a distinct decision, prepare and review the catalog record in the existing authoring workflow. Marking a name distinct alone does not add a catalog variety or prove breeder or ownership roles.

    A separately reviewed catalog record
  4. 4

    Build the profile

    Add cited traits, original patent links, credited photos and supported growing regions. Check missing fields in Coverage. A patent reference does not establish current rights; a photo reference may still need permission.

    A sourced profile with visible gaps

Keep the evidence attached: a publisher's claim stays attributed. Reviewing text, deciding a name and reviewing a statement are separate actions. Company operating regions do not establish where a particular variety grows.
